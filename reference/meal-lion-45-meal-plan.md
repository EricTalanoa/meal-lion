# Meal-lion: 45-meal content and asset plan

Working content proposal — October 16, 2026

## Recipe rules

These are defined game versions, not claims that every recipe uses exactly these ingredients. Lists contain 3–9 core clue ingredients (within the requested 3–10 range). Water, salt, routine frying oil and incidental garnish are generally omitted. Signature seasonings remain where they help identify the dish. Premade components such as pizza dough, curry paste and pastry count as one ingredient. Bread becomes croutons in Caesar salad; butter, egg and lemon become hollandaise in Eggs Benedict. Ingredient order below is inventory order, not final reveal order.

Use explicit recipe names where variants matter: beef Bolognese, beer-battered fish, chicken paella, bean-containing chili, and tofu Pad Thai. Accept spelling variants and common translations without treating genuinely different dishes as synonyms. This is a launch pool to playtest, not 45 fully balanced puzzles yet.

This does not require 103 separate generations: related ingredient icons can share source artwork, with variants where needed. Preserve text labels because rice varieties, stock types and some cheeses cannot be identified reliably from tiny pictures alone.

Existing draft atlas covers tomato, egg, bell pepper, onion, cumin and paprika. Its onion is purple; use that as the red-onion variant and make a neutral/yellow onion for the general Onion clue. Existing artwork should be audited before calling an ingredient final.

### Reuse families

- Rice: generic, basmati, short-grain and arborio; share bowl silhouette, vary grain shape and keep explicit labels.
- Stocks: shared carton or jug base with distinct vegetable, chicken and beef markers. Dashi gets its own label and visual treatment.
- Cheeses: block, wedge, crumble and soft-ball bases; distinct variants for cheddar, Parmesan, Pecorino, Gruyère, feta, mozzarella and paneer.
- Bread: sliced bread, burger bun, hot dog bun, English muffin and pita require recognizable silhouettes. Corn and flour tortillas share shape but differ in tone.
- Pasta: spaghetti, macaroni, lasagna sheets, generic pasta and rice noodles need separate shapes. Gnocchi is made from its ingredient clues and appears in the finished-meal art.
- Spices, oils and sauces: reusable jar, bottle and scoop bases; distinct contents and labels.
- Onion and red onion: shared silhouette, different skin and cut-face colors. Basil and Thai basil can share leaf shapes with a purple-stem variant.

Use a few reusable motions with ingredient props, plus signature reactions. Draft four-pose onion, tomato and egg sheets already exist. They need alignment cleanup and in-between frames before smooth production use.

Optional second-wave reactions: flour puff makes Leo blink; a peppery spice puff makes him sneeze away from the food; cheese stretches farther than expected; pancake flip lands on the plate; lemon makes him pucker. Keep these occasional so reveals stay quick.

## Per-meal asset contract

Each meal record references ingredient IDs, a manually ordered clue sequence, accepted answers, dish-family and cooking-method tags, one finished-meal sprite and a default reveal interaction. Each ingredient record references its icon, prop variants and Leo interaction ID. No unique background or full chef animation is required for each meal.

Example onion reveal: show onion icon → Leo chops → tears start → wipe eye → return to idle. Target 2–3 seconds, allow skipping, and show a still pose under reduced motion. Do not hide the clue until the animation ends.

## Daily and endless modes

- Both use the same meal records and artwork. Daily is one shared meal per calendar day with one saved result. Decide a single reset timezone before launch and display the local countdown.
- A 45-meal bank supplies 45 unique daily answers before reuse. Keep adding meals if long-term daily novelty is important.
- Endless uses a shuffled bag without repeats until the eligible pool is exhausted. Exclude today's daily meal before the player finishes it to avoid an immediate spoiler. Track endless results separately from daily streaks.
- Suggested six-attempt starting rule: reveal one ingredient initially; each wrong guess or skip reveals another until all ingredients are visible. A final wrong guess still ends the round. Short recipes stop adding ingredients and can supply a method/category clue; long recipes use six curated ingredient clues during play and show the remaining ingredients after the answer. Playtest this rule before locking it in.
- If every ingredient must be revealed during the round, instead use up to ten clue stages or reveal small groups. Do not let the current six-slot prototype silently dictate the final rules.

## Ambiguity and fairness checks

- Margherita vs pepperoni pizza: shared dough/tomato/cheese is intentionally insufficient. Ensure basil or pepperoni appears early enough to distinguish them.
- Butter chicken vs other tomato chicken curries: ingredients alone may not prove the exact name. Add preparation/style clues and define accepted answers deliberately.
- Pancakes vs other batters; French toast vs bread pudding; rösti vs hash browns: add shape or cooking-method clues when needed.
- Generic guesses such as “pizza”, “curry” or “pasta” should give category feedback, not consume a guess as if they were a specific wrong dish. Prefer searchable dish choices and accept spelling aliases.
- Do not label cottage pie as shepherd's pie: this entry uses beef. Use named variants consistently throughout clues, dropdowns and answer cards.
- Cuisine and dish families overlap; use curated tags instead of pretending there is one universal food taxonomy.

## Production batches

1. **Pilot:** shakshuka, pancakes, grilled cheese, spaghetti Bolognese, Greek salad, egg fried rice, miso soup, cheeseburger, butter chicken and fish and chips. Ten finished-meal sprites; generate missing icons for their ingredient union; polish onion, tomato and egg actions plus pour/sprinkle/stir/serve.
2. **Shared systems:** reusable prop placement, knife/board, pot/pan/bowl, pour stream, particles, tear loop, cloche and ingredient-to-interaction mapping.
3. **Remaining roster:** remaining 35 finished-meal sprites and remaining ingredient icons, grouped by asset family for consistency.
4. **Polish:** alignment, tiny-size readability, transparency, seamless loops, motion preferences, answer ambiguity and daily/endless replay testing.

Deliver individual PNG assets plus packed atlases and frame metadata. Keep equal frame bounds and anchor points for paws, board, knife and pot. Use one agreed pixel scale/palette; inspect at actual on-screen size. Existing generated sheets are style references and draft key poses, not evidence that pixel-perfect continuity is solved.
